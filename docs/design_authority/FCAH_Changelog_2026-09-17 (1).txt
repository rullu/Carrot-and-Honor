STATUS
The 2D / 2.5D world-map settlement experiment is concluded successfully enough to move on. The accepted prototype direction is a transparent 2D settlement miniature placed in world space with proper grounding, normal depth interaction, and restrained strategic exaggeration.

This is a prototype acceptance, not approval of the current test artwork as final production art. The current image, "The Victim", contains obvious generated-art defects such as incomplete/awkward wall continuity and is only being used to validate scale, composition, hierarchy, silhouette, and map readability.

ACCEPTED VISUAL DIRECTION
Settlements should read as compact strategic miniatures in the spirit of classic Knights of Honor 1 / Knights of Honor 2 world-map settlement presentation.

Core rule:
Settlement importance must come from footprint, hierarchy, silhouette, landmarks, and structured density — not from oversized ordinary buildings or from crushing every element into a dense blob.

The desired middle ground is:
- compact enough to read as one cohesive strategic object
- substantial enough to feel important and worth fighting over
- internally readable rather than visually crushed
- not realistically spread across a huge geographic footprint
- not a circular or emblem-like pile of buildings
- strong enough that even the lowest development stage retains meaningful visual presence

PROVISIONAL SETTLEMENT DESIGN RULES
These rules are now accepted as the working settlement-composition standard and should be carried into later documentation/design records:

1. House scale remains broadly believable and consistent between development stages. Larger or richer settlements gain presence through more buildings, stronger structures, larger footprint, and stronger landmarks rather than inflated house size.

2. Every settlement needs visible breathing room. Streets, alleys, courtyards, market space, small gardens, and gaps between building clusters must remain readable.

3. Use a clear hierarchy. A settlement should normally have one dominant seat-of-power element — manor, keep, castle, hall, fortified tower, or equivalent depending on culture and development — with secondary civic/religious landmarks beneath it.

4. Castle / seat of power should be spatially related to the town but not mashed into it. It may sit uphill, toward one edge, behind an inner wall, or overlook the settlement. It should not consume the whole composition.

5. The outer silhouette should be irregular and recognizable rather than a perfect circle or square. Organic asymmetry is desirable because it helps settlements remain identifiable at distance.

6. Development stages should change structure and hierarchy, not merely size. Even a low-development settlement must feel like a place worth fighting over rather than a few insignificant huts lost inside a province.

7. The core settlement asset remains separate from modular province sites. Farms, monasteries, mines, quarries, harbors, and other external sites should remain separate assets/systems rather than being permanently fused into the town image.

8. Fixed-camera art direction is acceptable. Settlement assets are authored for the game's approximately 40-degree oblique strategy-camera language; arbitrary camera rotation is not required.

9. Strong readability at close/default gameplay zoom is mandatory. Extreme far-zoom readability should not be solved by endlessly increasing physical settlement scale. If later needed, zoom-dependent presentation or LOD can be considered separately.

10. No clipping or accidental visual cutoff is acceptable. Assets need sufficient transparent padding, and the Sprite3D presentation must ground the visible bottom of the artwork correctly at terrain height.

DENSITY / COMPOSITION TARGET
The experiment established that both extremes fail:
- earlier castle-heavy image: too cramped / too much visual mass packed together
- later broad image: too spread out / read more like a realistic city layout than one strategic-map object

The accepted target is the middle ground: a compact irregular settlement with readable internal structure.

A useful art-direction guide is approximately 70–75% built/structured visual mass and 25–30% readable open space. This is not a mathematical runtime rule; it is a visual design check.

A practical target for a medium prototype composition is roughly 20–30 houses/workshops plus a dominant seat of power, one secondary civic/religious landmark, and a readable market/town-center structure. Exact counts may vary by region and development stage.

FINAL BORDER TARGET
The desired final direction is a two-sided political border band rather than a
single thin center line.

Design intent:
- the mathematically true province boundary remains the center seam
- each adjacent province contributes one separate colored half-band
- both half-bands meet exactly at the actual border
- each band extends slightly inward into its own province
- color is strongest/readable at the shared seam
- opacity/color softly weakens toward the province interior
- subtle hatching / brushed / lightly striped texture gives the bands a painted
  medieval-map character rather than a clean modern UI strip
- bands should be broader and clearly readable at normal and far gameplay zoom
- presentation must remain visible without overpowering the terrain
- colors should use a restrained medieval / heraldic palette:
  faded crimson, dusty blue, moss/sage green, muted ochre, weathered plum,
  iron grey-blue, warm brown-red, and similar desaturated earthy pigments
- no neon colors
- no glow
- no bright hover outline
- no persistent selection outline
- no separate coastline border

Long-term color architecture should allow each side of a shared border to receive
a realm/province color dynamically rather than hard-coding one universal border
color. This will allow the same rendering system to support the future political
realm map.

GAMEPLAY STATE ARCHITECTURE V1 — DESIGN LOCK
Date: 2026-09-17
======================================================================

STATUS
The first authoritative gameplay-state architecture for FCAH is now CLOSED and
LOCKED as the foundation for future campaign simulation work.

Primary authority:
- docs/design_authority/FCAH_Gameplay_State_Architecture_v1_LOCKED.txt

This combined architecture consolidates the previously closed Province/Realm,
RealmState, CharacterState, DynastyState and RelationshipState design work into
one implementation authority.

CENTRAL ARCHITECTURE RULE
Store each fact on the entity or subsystem that actually owns that fact.
Derive it everywhere else.

The campaign must not grow into one giant god-object or into several competing
sources of truth.

CORE AUTHORITATIVE STATE OBJECTS
The five closed core state objects are:
- ProvinceState
- RealmState
- CharacterState
- DynastyState
- RelationshipState

Known separate/satellite boundaries include:
- WarState
- TradeRouteState
- ExpeditionState
- TreasuryCrisisState when needed
- RebellionState when needed
- SiegeState when needed
- future Marshal / Army / Formation objects
- future Institution / Tradition representations

No universal ProjectState or generic catch-all state object is authorized.

SOURCE-OF-TRUTH RULES
Current ownership:
- Province.owner_realm_id is authoritative.
- Realm territory is derived from Province ownership.

Capital:
- Realm.capital_province_id is authoritative.

Current ruler:
- Realm.current_ruler_id is authoritative.
- CharacterState owns the ruler rabbit's personal facts.

Dynasty membership:
- Character.dynasty_id is authoritative.
- Dynasty member lists and current ruling dynasty are derived.

Current war:
- WarState is authoritative.
- RelationshipState does not duplicate an "at war" truth.

Bilateral diplomacy:
- one canonical RelationshipState exists per Realm pair.
- directional attitudes can differ inside that one object.

Claims:
- claims may belong to a Character, Dynasty or Realm.
- each claim record has exactly one authoritative claimant.
- related rights may share provenance, but not duplicate authority.

History:
- Province, Realm, Character, Dynasty and Relationship may each retain compact
  perspective-specific historical memory.
- historical memory must never be treated as current gameplay truth.

STABLE ID / LIFECYCLE RULES
Stable IDs are opaque identities and must not be inferred from:
- array index
- display order
- current owner
- name
- style
- active/inactive state

Historical IDs are not recycled.

Realm lifecycle:
- active Realm: owns at least one Province
- active Realm: exactly one owned capital and one current ruler
- NPC Realm with zero Provinces: becomes inactive but remains historically
  resolvable
- player Realm with zero Provinces: campaign ends
- formable transformation: same stable Realm ID, new political identity
- restoration: reactivates the same historical Realm ID
- genuinely new rebel Realm: receives a new ID

Character lifecycle:
- deceased persistent Characters remain resolvable
- important Characters are persistent; narrative-only names do not need full
  CharacterState
- parent links are the recommended authoritative family edge
- children are derived
- spouse/partner links must be reciprocal and validated

Dynasty lifecycle:
- Dynasty identity is independent from any Realm
- no living members means extinct/inactive, but the Dynasty remains historical
- marriage does not merge Dynasties or automatically rewrite dynasty membership

PROVINCE / REALM DESIGN BOUNDARIES PRESERVED
Province:
- one current owner
- one primary settlement / management hub
- local population/manpower/food reality
- local culture/religion
- development and later local conditions/systems
- no generic wartime Controller for normal conquest
- ownership changes directly when a city/province is captured

Realm:
- owns political identity, capital, ruler/heir roles, official identity,
  Realm-wide Carrots and Faith, laws/policies and Realm claims/history
- Food and manpower are not Realm wallets; they are derived from local state
- current ruling Dynasty is derived through current ruler
- style is not inferred from Realm size
- ordinary conquest does not automatically rename/promote a Realm

RELATIONSHIP DESIGN
One canonical RelationshipState is retained for each Realm pair, including
historical/inactive Realms.

RelationshipState may own bilateral agreements and compact relationship memory.
It does not own:
- active-war truth
- Realm claims
- character opinions
- a universal single "relation score"

DYNAMIC DIPLOMACY PRINCIPLE
History and identity may bias diplomacy, probabilities and reactions, but current
campaign diplomacy is not a fixed script.

TRANSACTION PRINCIPLE
Multi-entity changes such as:
- province capture
- capital relocation
- Realm inactivity
- restoration
- rebel Realm creation
- formable transformation
- succession

must be applied coherently and validated as one state transition rather than
publishing partially changed live state.

DESIGN AUDIT
The combined architecture audit passed with no hard contradiction found between
the closed state models.

The architecture deliberately leaves detailed future mechanics such as full
economy, combat, AI, authored succession systems, building systems, traditions,
institutions and detailed diplomacy logic for later subsystem passes.

FINAL DESIGN DECISION
Gameplay State Architecture v1 is accepted as the authoritative state ownership
and lifecycle foundation for FCAH.

Future systems should attach to this architecture rather than invent parallel
ownership/state models.

2. Claim-target coercion risk
An early claim-loading path could convert an invalid fractional Province target

Fix:
- registry keys are validated against record identities before session creation,
  publication and save

Identical authority files could hash differently on Windows and Linux because of
CRLF versus LF line endings.

- actual source files are not rewritten

STATUS
A small Codex-driven visual inspection pass was completed after Logic Foundation

This handover is intentionally recorded in the cumulative changelog because code,

POLITICAL MAP MODE v0.1 — INTERNAL INSPECTION TOOL
Codex implemented a temporary Political inspection overlay in Gameplay 001.

Current behavior:
- P toggles Normal <-> Political mode.
- The existing Terrain3D world remains visible.
- A 38% Realm-color tint is applied through a derived Province-ID mask.
- Current ownership is read from ProvinceState.owner_realm_id.
- All Provinces owned by the same Realm receive the same political color.
- Starting Realm colors are deterministic and optimized for neighboring contrast.
- Existing terrain, water, vegetation, settlements, accepted borders and camera are
  preserved.
- Normal mode restores the original terrain shader and border presentation.

The implementation produced a reusable presentation seam rather than a duplicate
political source of truth. A live CampaignSession can supply current ownership;
the standalone inspection scene seeds current ProvinceState ownership from frozen
starting ownership until the authored production scenario exists.

Political Realm-name labels were added as a second small pass:
- one label per currently landed Realm
- authoritative Realm/formable display identity
- territorial anchors derived from currently owned Provinces
- labels refresh after campaign ownership/identity changes
- labels disappear in Normal mode
- labels avoid the existing inspection panel at overview scale

Known accepted weakness:
- label readability/scaling is imperfect at mid and closer zoom ranges
- continent/far-overview readability is better than some intermediate zoom levels
- this is accepted because the mode is currently for internal inspection only

The intended final player-facing Political Realm View remains a future redesign.
That later system is expected to use the exact terrain camera angle but a distinct
political presentation layer, strong Realm presentation, larger overview zoom,
proper typography, eventual parchment treatment and a polished terrain-to-map
transition. The current tint overlay must not be mistaken for that final design.

CULTURE MAP MODE v0.1 — INTERNAL INSPECTION TOOL
Codex then implemented a temporary Culture inspection mode by reusing the accepted
Province-mask/tint presentation path.

Current behavior:
- C toggles Normal <-> Culture mode.
- P and C switch directly between mutually exclusive Political and Culture modes.
- Only one map mode is active at a time.
- Culture is read from the Province-level authoritative field
  ProvinceState.local_culture.
- Canonical campaign bootstrap already seeds local culture from validated World
  Identity Province culture.
- The standalone inspection scene seeds that same field from canonical starting
  identity until a live CampaignSession is bound.
- Realm primary culture and Realm ownership do not decide Province culture color.
- All Provinces sharing a culture receive the same deterministic color across Realm
  borders.
- Existing Province boundaries remain readable.
- Culture labels are placed per connected cultural region rather than forcing one
  misleading label between disconnected areas of the same culture.

Starting-state inspection facts verified by the implementation:
- 100 active Provinces
- 18 starting cultures
- 28 connected culture regions

KNOWN LIMITATIONS / DEFERRED PRESENTATION WORK
The temporary Culture view is accepted as useful but visually incomplete.

Known limitations:
- no culture legend yet
- zoom/readability of labels is functional rather than polished
- culture is represented primarily through color separation
- no richer hierarchical map grammar is implemented yet

The missing legend is explicitly acknowledged and should be added only when map
presentation is revisited; current Codex usage was intentionally not spent polishing
this temporary tool further.

FAITH / RELIGION MAP — DEFERRED DIRECTION
A Faith/Religion inspection map was discussed but not implemented in this pass.

Preferred future visual grammar:
- broad/main religion represented primarily by base fill color
- rite / branch / communion distinctions represented through secondary visual
  encoding such as stripes, hatching, dots, value variation or restrained border
  treatment rather than assigning every lower-level religious identity an unrelated
  base color
- a proper hierarchical legend is mandatory

This should allow the player/developer to read broad religious families first and
then see internal rites/branches without destroying visual kinship.
